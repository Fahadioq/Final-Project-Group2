# Cross-Generator Generalization in AI-Generated Image Detection

DATS 6303: Deep Learning and Fundamentals of AI

Group 2: Fahad Aloqayli, Muhannad Alhamoudi, and Alexander D. Silberman

## Problem and Motivation

Generative models such as Stable Diffusion and Midjourney now produce photorealistic images that can be misused for misinformation. Neural networks can learn to separate real from generated images, but detectors trained on one generator often fail on generators unseen during training. Because new generators appear continually, this weakness limits practical use. Our goal is to measure the effectiveness of CNNs trained on one subset of generators against those they have yet to see.

## Dataset

We will use OpenFake (Livernoche et al., 2025) v.2.0, a public (CC-BY-NC 4.0) dataset and benchmark for detecting deepfakes (or, more generally, AI-generated images, particularly of political or current-affairs content) comprising approximately 2.8 million images generated from a wide variety of both synthetic and real-world sources. Synthetic sources include frontier proprietary model families like OpenAI's, Google's, and Midjourney's; open-source model families like Stable Diffusion and Flux; various fine-tuned open-source models; and frames extracted from videos generated by various models. Real-world sources include LAION, Pexels, DOCCI, and ImageNet.

At 3.97 TB, the dataset is far larger than either the VRAM available or the disk space; as such, we will deal solely with smaller subsets, only with cropped (256x256) images, and will have to preprocess these by streaming parquet shards.

## Framework
PyTorch and torchvision. They provide pretrained weights, mixed-precision GPU training, and transparent training loops we can modify and explain.

## Model

Various pre-trained, but primarily ResNet-50. The output layer will have to be modified, but the structure will be kept largely the same.

### Input

OpenFake includes a prompt alongside each image, generated with an LLM for those without prompts. For the purposes of this experiment, these prompts will be discarded, for two reasons: 1) in the real world, it is often that you will have to judge from the image itself, and 2) learning to identify LLM-generated text instead of determining whether an image is real or fake would result in a good metric but a poor result.

### Output

Sigmoids for Real/Fake and a Softmax over all known generators, `unknown-generator`, all known real sources, and `unknown-real`.

## Metrics
F1, primarily, but also the true positive rate at a set 5% false positive rate, and AUC-ROC.
